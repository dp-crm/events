Things to look at

    How this was worked out

    Step 1. Your monthly expense today, less the expenses that stop before you retire, is grown by inflation up to your retirement age. That gives your monthly expense at retirement. Dependants add 5% (one) or 10% (two) to it, every year of retirement.

    Step 2. From retirement to age 90, you withdraw that yearly expense at the start of each year. It rises 5% a year with inflation, while the money left earns 8%. The amount needed on the day you retire, to pay for all those years, is your corpus.

    Step 3. Rs. 25 lakh is added and kept aside as a health emergency fund. That total is your FIRE number.

    What changes your FIRE number

    • Monthly expenses: the more you spend each year, the larger the corpus, since your investments must support those expenses throughout retirement.
    • Retirement age: retiring early means a longer retirement and a bigger corpus.
    • Inflation: rising prices raise your future living costs, so the corpus must keep your purchasing power.
    • Investment returns: better returns can get you to the goal faster. Lower returns mean saving more.
    • Lifestyle choices: travel, hobbies, housing and dining plans decide what you spend after retiring.
    • Healthcare costs: medical expenses and insurance premiums can add a lot, so keep a buffer.
    • Dependants: if you will still support children, parents or others, their expenses count too.
    • Debt and liabilities: loans and EMIs raise expenses and can delay your FIRE date if not managed well.

    Markets do not give fixed returns. This is a planning estimate, to be reviewed every year.
